PIXEL DECISION.

Today’s question: can a simple colour rule isolate the blue tile while ignoring the green triangle, yellow circle, and dark stone? A mask answers once for every pixel: keep or ignore.

5–11 MIN · CHOOSE A RULE

HSV GIVES
THREE CLUES.

Hue is the colour family. Saturation is how strong that colour is. Value is how bright it is. A blue tile in shadow may keep its hue while losing value; a bright reflection may lose saturation.

H · HUEIs it in the blue family?
S · SATURATIONIs the colour strong enough?
V · VALUEIs it bright enough to trust?

11–20 MIN · BUILD + INSPECT

THE RULE MAKES
A RAW MASK.

The notebook converts the image from RGB to HSV, then checks whether every pixel falls between two blue limits. The first result is a raw mask: useful evidence, but not yet a trustworthy object.

hsv  = cv2.cvtColor(image, cv2.COLOR_RGB2HSV)
mask = cv2.inRange(hsv, LOWER_BLUE, UPPER_BLUE)

20–29 MIN · CLEAN WITH A REASON

REMOVE SPECKS.
KEEP EVIDENCE.

Mask pixels can be scattered or have small holes. Opening removes isolated specks. Closing fills small holes. Both operations change the evidence, so use the smallest change that fixes a visible problem.

OPENUse when tiny matching pixels are not part of the object.
CLOSEUse when one object has small gaps inside it.
EXPLAINIf opening deletes part of your tile, should you use a larger or smaller kernel? What observation supports your choice?

38–45 MIN · STRESS THE RULE

LIGHT CHANGES
THE EVIDENCE.

Keep the object fixed, then compare normal light, shadow, and glare. A failed mask tells you which condition your rule cannot handle yet. Change one HSV bound at a time so the cause stays clear.
